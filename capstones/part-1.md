# PART 1 CAPSTONE — Theoretical Shop Catalogue — relational literacy

**Status:** NOT STARTED  
**Rule:** capstone must be demonstrated as functioning **within its defined learning scope** before advancing to Part 2.  
**Notes:** open-note allowed; retries documented; learner is the implementer, and may not use AI-generated code before P3M3.

## Deliverables / observable behaviors

- Explain a synthetic print-on-demand shop schema in Markdown or a hand-drawn ERD.
- Distinguish `designs`, `products`, `suppliers`, `listings`, `mock_orders`, `order_items`, `fulfillment_events`, and `audit_events`.
- Write **your own** SQLite-compatible `CREATE TABLE`, basic insert/update/select/join/index examples and explain their purpose.
- Use fake names, identifiers and addresses. Show a constraint violation, a simple query plan explanation, and a throwaway-file backup/restore.
- Explain the hypothetical process from listing to fulfillment without enabling any external store, payment or order.
- **No actual operational shop backend is required**; passing means the schema model and synthetic demonstration are coherent, reproducible and explained.
- Verify the Part 1 localhost sandbox/file-sharing safety gate before advancing.

## Submission package

- Learner's own explanation of the system and decisions
- Evidence folder or a clear commit reference
- Test plan, reproducible test output and failure/recovery notes
- Source citations in the SQL tree when appropriate
- Security-gate decision and retest evidence, if applicable

## Evaluation and demonstration

The exact demonstration script and module-specific standards will be agreed during this part. A polished presentation is optional; **function, explanation, reproducibility and relevant safety** are essential.

**Noncritical gap:** log for follow-up.  
**Blocking gap:** required operation not demonstrated, or a critical safety boundary failed. Fix and retest.
