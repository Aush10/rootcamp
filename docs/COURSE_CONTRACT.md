# Rootcamp course contract

## Why this is a crash course

The modern technical ecosystem is too large to master exhaustively before making useful systems. Each module teaches enough to **recognize the layer, operate the tool, explain its boundaries, and recover a failure**. Rabbit holes go on a backlog unless they are safety-critical.

## Each subject must contain

1. **Required human reading:** one short, clearly identified piece by a named human author with relevant domain authority. Occasionally two, for contested claims or critical security nuance. Include link/book source, pages or section, expected reading minutes, and verification status.
2. **Recommended reading:** generally 2–4 credible follow-ups, not mandatory.
3. **AI overview:** bounded explanation of what it is, why Rootcamp cares, where it fits, what it does *not* do, and a security or operational implication.
4. **Ungraded assignment:** directly interact with the subject (guided action, safe experiment, local build, research comparison, or short trial); no score and no punitive gating.
5. **Artifacts and links:** what to save, related prior/future topics, and optional rabbit holes.

### Subject assignment versus module assessment

A *subject assignment* is practice. It does **not** require scoring, approval or a passing threshold.

A *module assessment* happens **after the module's subjects**. It evaluates functional competence through an open-note prompt, demonstrable output, or artifact review. It may be numeric, descriptive, or a reproducible test. Its form should match the topic; do not force every module into a multiple-choice examination.

A *part capstone* is a functioning final artifact/demonstration and is required to move to the next part. Safety-critical shortcomings must be repaired before proceeding; less serious shortcomings are recorded and revisited.

## Assessment principles

- Open notes, official documentation, and previous artifacts are allowed.
- Before P3M3: **no AI-generated student code, patch instructions, or direct solution**. AI may review completed work and ask clarifying questions without providing substitute code.
- Peer/reviewer critique is evidence-based; reviewer must distinguish **confirmed defect**, **unverified risk**, and **style preference**.
- Modules may use percent, checklist, oral defense, or deployment demonstration.
- Learner can revise and resubmit without penalty for the extra attempt; save the first failure and repair in the learning journal.
- Proposed 70/100 applies only where a numeric score is useful. It never excuses a critical vulnerability or a broken capstone.
- Any automated assistant is an **advisor**, never sole authority for a high-risk system's safety.

## Smallest credible evidence set

- What you tried and why
- Source/reference used and what you learned
- Files/commands/screenshots that prove the attempt
- What failed, and how you debugged or reverted
- A human-authored explanation of the result
- Module assessment result and next action

## Personal autonomy

This is the user's curriculum. The course does not assume that AI should make live purchases, change security settings, manage real credentials, or deploy on the student's behalf. Those require explicit separate authorization and safety checks.
