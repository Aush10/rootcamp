# Reading source policy

## Required source criteria

- **Human authored:** an identifiable human author or clearly identified set of human editors/maintainers with subject expertise. Prefer a specific article, chapter or short official explanation; a search engine summary is not a reading.
- **Authority:** relevant practitioner, contributor, standards editor, maintainer, researcher, or recognized technical educator.
- **Short:** a coherent 5–20 minute selection. If the topic needs more, split across subjects rather than stuffing a module.
- **Accessible:** link to the official/author-hosted page, institutional library, stable open textbook or accurately identified book chapter.
- **Traceable:** save title, author, original date or edition if known, access date, verified URL status, and notes on reliability/limitations.
- **Fair representation:** distinguish standards, empirical findings, professional advice, marketing, and speculation.

## Recommended reading criteria

Usually 2–4 sources, mixing practical docs, conceptual explanation, and security or historical context when useful. Optional sources should not explode the timebox.

## Verification

At course-authoring time, **check citations and links in the live session** where web verification is available. An unverified link is explicitly marked as such and should not be represented as verified. No fabricated DOI or claimed access date.

## For historical and fast-changing AI topics

Anchor in earlier documented developments, then distinguish:
- historical milestones;
- current products/capabilities that must be checked when taught;
- plausible technical directions;
- vendor promises or hype (not facts).
